## Keynote A (Time: 9:15-10:15)

**Title**: Toward Cultural Alignment: Human-Centered Evaluation of Multimodal AI Stories Across Five African Communities

**Speakers**: <a href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/people/mochieng/">Millicent Ochieng</a> and <a href="https://felerminoali.github.io/">Felermino Ali</a>

**Abstract**: Generative AI systems can produce fluent and locally plausible content while still failing to reflect the lived practices, relationships, language, values, and visual expectations of the communities they represent. In this talk, we present a community-grounded mixed-methods evaluation of multimodal AI-generated stories across five African communities. We show that cultural alignment depends not simply on recognizable cultural markers, but on how those markers fit social, linguistic, procedural, and visual context. We introduce a taxonomy of five broader cultural marker categories and eight recurring mechanisms of misalignment, and examine whether multimodal LLM judges can approximate community-grounded judgments at scale. Our findings show substantial variation in judge reliability and calibration across communities, motivating community-calibrated evaluation in which automated judges are validated against community judgments rather than assumed to be universally reliable. More broadly, we argue that building more representative AI requires not only representing diverse languages and communities, but also involving those communities in defining whether that representation is actually culturally aligned.

**Bio**:

Millicent Ochieng is a Research Scientist at Microsoft Research Africa and a PhD researcher in Computer Science at the University of the Witwatersrand. Her research focuses on multilingual and multicultural AI, with particular interests in multilingual tokenization, cultural alignment, multimodal AI, AI evaluation, and equitable language technologies. Her work explores how AI systems can better represent linguistic and cultural diversity across global and underrepresented contexts.

Felermino Ali is a Postdoctoral Researcher at Microsoft Research Africa in Nairobi, specializing in Artificial Intelligence and Natural Language Processing. He holds a PhD in Informatics Engineering from the University of Porto, where his research focused on machine translation for low-resource languages. His research interests include multilingual AI, machine translation, ASR, language models, tokenization, AI evaluation, and language technologies for underrepresented African languages.

## Keynote B (Time: 16:00-17:00)

**Title**: Evaluating Multimodal Models on Cultural Question Answering in Low-Resource Languages

**Speaker**: <a href="https://atnafuatx.github.io/">Atnafu Lambebo Tonja</a>

**Abstract**: Africa is home to over one-third of the world’s languages, yet remains severely underrepresented in multimodal AI research. We introduce Afri-MCQA, the first multilingual cultural question-answering benchmark with 7.5k QA pairs across 15 African languages from 12 countries. The benchmark offers parallel text and speech modalities and was entirely created by native speakers. We find that models perform poorly across evaluated cultures, with near-zero accuracy on open-ended VQA when queried in the native language or via speech. To test linguistic competence, we include control experiments to assess this aspect separately from cultural knowledge, and we observe significant performance gaps between native languages and English for both text and speech. These findings underscore the pressing need for speech-first approaches, culturally grounded pretraining, and cross-lingual cultural transfer. We release Afri-MCQA to support more inclusive multimodal AI development.

**Bio**: Atnafu Lambebo Tonja is a Senior Research Fellow (Google DeepMind Academic Fellow) at University College London (UCL). Previously, he was a postdoctoral researcher at Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI) in the UAE. He holds a PhD in Computer Science from Instituto Politécnico Nacional, Mexico, focusing on neural machine translation for low-resource languages. His research advances NLP for underrepresented languages, particularly African and Ethiopian languages, by developing multilingual language models and sustainable data curation frameworks. He has published work on low-resource languages, especially African and Ethiopian languages, in top NLP venues including ACL, EMNLP, and NAACL.

## Panel Discussion (Time: 11:00-12:30)

**Topic**: Sovereign AI: Must We Own the Model to Own Our AI Future?

The panel brings together researchers working across different regions and perspectives to discuss the growing importance of AI sovereignty, including topics such as local language and cultural representation, data and model ownership, technological independence, open and locally developed AI ecosystems, and the challenges of building AI systems that reflect the needs and values of different communities.

**Panelists**:

- <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/raymond-ng-ai/">Raymond Ng</a>

Raymond Ng is an AI engineer with the SEA-LION team in AI Products, AI Singapore. Raymond mainly contributes to pre-training and continued pre-training effort for the SEA-LION project (LLM and embedding models) as well as research work on improving multilingual large language models and embedding models.

- <a href="https://www.fajrikoto.com/">Fajri Koto</a>

Fajri Koto is an Assistant Professor at MBZUAI and is currently a Visiting Scholar at the University of Cambridge. He leads the Koto MOSAIC Lab, where his research centers on human-centered NLP, including cultural alignment of large language models, safety and fairness in generative AI, agent-based NLP applications, reasoning models, and efficient multilingual NLP for low-resource languages. He has contributed to the development of language models for Southeast Asia and the Arab region, including JAIS, one of the largest Arabic LLMs. He has published extensively at top venues such as ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, NeurIPS, and ICLR, and his work has received an Outstanding Paper Award at EACL 2023, a Resource Paper Award at AACL 2023, and a Best Paper Award for Social Impact at an ICML 2026 workshop.

María Grandury is a PhD student at EPFL NLP, where she works on multilingual LLM evaluation. She has co-created the regional knowledge benchmark “INCLUDE”, accepted at this conference, and contributes to Apertus, Switzerland’s sovereign AI model, designed to be transparent, compliant, and multilingual. She is a Hugging Face Fellow and the founder of SomosNLP, an international Ibero-American community dedicated to multicultural open-source NLP. María holds a double degree in Mathematics and Physics and a master’s degree in Language Technologies. Before her PhD, she worked at startups in Berlin and Madrid evaluating AI quality and reliability. Alongside research and community initiatives, María enjoys giving lectures, keynotes, and participating in panels like today’s to promote inclusive AI.

His current research spans multilingual evaluation, culturally grounded synthetic data, and understanding disparities in the capabilities of language models across languages and communities. Prior to joining Microsoft Research, he completed his PhD at IIIT Hyderabad, where his research focused on code-mixed NLP, including datasets, evaluation, and adaptation of multilingual models for English–Hindi code-mixed text. He is a recipient of the Microsoft Research India PhD Award 2024.
